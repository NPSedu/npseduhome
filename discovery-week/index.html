🖼️ ARCADE STATION CHECKLIST

Before 11:20am, prepare your display station with the following:

☐ Game is open and ready to play on screen
☐ A title sign or label showing your game's name
☐ A short instruction card (max 5 steps - how to play)
☐ Your name is clearly visible
☐ Volume is set appropriately (not too loud!)

📝 Writing Your Instruction Card

Write 3–5 simple steps explaining how to play. Make it clear for someone who has never seen your game before.

Example:

  1. Press the space bar to start
  2. Use arrow keys to move your character
  3. Collect all the stars before the timer runs out
  4. Avoid the red enemies - they reset your progress
  5. Reach the door to complete the level!

🔊 Sound & Music

🎸

GarageBand

Available on iPad and iMac

GarageBand is your main music and sound effects tool. Use it to create original soundtracks and atmospheric audio for your game.

Quick Tips for Game Audio

  • Loops work best - Create a 15–30 second loop that repeats seamlessly as background music
  • Simple is better - A few well-chosen instruments beat a cluttered track
  • Match the mood - Fast BPM = action, slow BPM = exploration, minor key = danger
  • Export as MP3 - Share > Song to Files, then import into your game tool
  • Sound effects - Use GarageBand's built-in sound library for jump, collect, and win sounds
  • Record yourself - You can record voice-over narration directly on iPad

AI GUIDE

🤖 Using AI This Week

AI is your development assistant - not your game developer. Here's everything you need to know.

THE CORE RULE

AI is a tool. You are the creator. Your ideas, your decisions, your game — AI just helps you get there.

✅ WHAT YOU MAY USE AI FOR

🐛

Debugging

Paste your code and describe the problem. Ask AI to help find the bug. You still need to understand and apply the fix.

🧪

Testing

Ask AI what edge cases might break your game. What happens if a player does something unexpected?

✨

Improving Code

Ask AI if there's a more efficient or readable way to write something you've already built.

💬

Feedback

Describe a mechanic and ask AI what could make it more fun, fair, or interesting for players.

❌ WHAT YOU MAY NOT USE AI FOR

✗

Generating your whole game

Don't ask AI to create your game from scratch. The whole point is that YOU build it.

✗

Making creative decisions

Don't ask AI "what should my game be about?" or "what characters should I design?" Those decisions belong to you.

✗

Planning your story

Your story and world were built on Day 1 - by you. AI shouldn't rewrite your creative foundation.

✗

Replacing your thinking

If you're stuck, try to work it out yourself first. Use AI as a last resort for creative blocks, not a first stop.

💬 EXAMPLE PROMPTS

Copy and adapt these prompts when you use AI. Good prompts = better help.

✅ GOOD PROMPT
"In Scratch, my character falls through the floor instead of landing on it. Here is my code: [paste]. The platform is coloured blue. Can you help me figure out why the 'touching colour blue' block isn't stopping the fall?"
Why it works: specific problem, your own code, clear question
❌ BAD PROMPT
"Make me a platformer game in Scratch."
Why it doesn't work: this asks AI to do the work for you - that's not allowed and won't help you learn
✅ GOOD PROMPT
"My game mechanic is: the player has to dodge falling rocks while collecting coins. What are some ways players might find this unfair or frustrating? What should I test to make it feel balanced?"
Why it works: your mechanic, asking for testing ideas, keeping creative control
❌ BAD PROMPT
"What should my game be about? Give me a good story idea."
Why it doesn't work: this is a creative decision that must come from you, not AI

SUCCESS CRITERIA

🏆 Success Criteria

Games are judged across seven domains. Know these from day one - build with intention.

These criteria are shared with students from the very start of the week so that every decision - design, coding, visuals, sound - can be made with the showcase in mind.

🎨

Game Design & Idea

  • Clear objective - the player knows what to do
  • Creativity and originality - the idea is the student's own
  • Appropriate for the audience
🖥️

Coding & Functionality

  • Reliable gameplay - no major crashes or broken mechanics
  • Use of coding concepts (loops, conditions, variables, events)
  • Working controls
  • Progression and challenge
🎭

Visual Design & Creativity

  • Effective sprites and backgrounds
  • Strong visual communication
  • Design consistency - it looks intentional
🔊

Sound & Engagement

  • Purposeful sound and music
  • Audio enhances the overall experience
🕹️

User Experience

  • Clear instructions - a new player knows how to start
  • Playability - it's fun to play
  • Replayability - there's a reason to play again
  • Accessibility - different players can engage with it
🔬

Testing & Improvement

  • Evidence of iterative testing
  • Bug fixing shown in development log
  • The game improved over the week
📖

Best Character & Storyline

  • Characters are original, memorable, and well-developed
  • The story has a clear beginning, challenge, and resolution
  • World-building adds depth to the player experience
  • Story and characters are reflected in the game's design
