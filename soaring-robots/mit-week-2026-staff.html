The Week

The week, minute by minute

Tap a day to see the full run of show — timings, locations, and exactly what to do. Checkpoints are highlighted in colour.

8:20–8:30 Tutor Rooms

Registration

Register students; confirm each student knows their team and base room; cover any housekeeping (dress code, week overview).

Students: Register with tutors; find out which team and base room you're in.

8:30–9:00 Base Room

Team Bases & Team Welcome

Welcome students into base rooms; check the right students are in the right place; introduce yourself and settle the room.

Students: Find your base room and team; meet your teammates.

9:00–10:00 The Globe

Launch Assembly

At least one facilitator per team should attend. Staff new to MIT Week should definitely be there. This session also covers instructions for The Great MIT Supply Drop team-building challenge.

Students: Sit with your team; watch the launch, find out about the Soaring Robots challenge and Professor Zufferey, and hear the instructions for The Great MIT Supply Drop.

10:00–10:20 · Break
10:20–11:20 Base Room

The Great MIT Supply Drop — Build

Distribute materials kits and payload; run the one-hour build using the Student Instruction Sheet and Facilitator Slides. Circulate, encourage testing and iteration, and note the teamwork and planning/improvement points (30 of the 100-point score) as you observe.

Students: As a team, design, build and test your supply-drop capsule within the hour. Use the Test Record on the back of your instruction sheet. Capsule must be finished, labelled and ready for inspection by 11:20.

11:20–12:10 The Globe

The Great MIT Supply Drop — Live Drop Event

Lead teams to The Globe with their finished capsules (queue by House); run the live drop as host, balcony launch supervisor, queue manager, landing marshal, payload inspector or scorekeeper (~45 sec/team). Announce each result and award House points immediately.

Students: Carry your capsule carefully to The Globe. Watch your team's capsule released from the balcony onto the target, and celebrate the result — success or not.

12:10–12:20+ The Globe

Challenge Launch — Full Brief Reveal

Facilitators in The Globe give a fast, detailed reveal of the Soaring Robots brief, judging criteria and challenge details. It's fine if this runs a few minutes past 12:20 into lunch — let students know the full brief will be covered again after lunch (1:40–3:20), when they'll have time to ask questions.

Students: Watch the detailed reveal of Soaring Robots in The Globe — brief, judging criteria and more. Don't worry about catching everything: your facilitator will go through it all again after lunch.

12:20–1:20 · Lunch
1:20–1:40 Base Room

Team Roles

Present Facilitator Slides: "Roles Give Ownership, Not Silos." Help each team allocate all 9 roles.

Students: Choose/allocate roles (Project Manager, Biology Lead, Engineering Lead, Design Lead, Prototype Lead, Innovation Lead, Communications Lead, Videographer, Expo Stall Manager). Record on Team Workbook cover (p.1).

1:40–3:20 Base Room

Challenge Introduction (detailed) + Define + Learn & Explore

Begin with a detailed walkthrough of the Soaring Robots brief, official design criteria and judging rubric (Facilitator Slides: "What Every Robot Must Do", "How Judges Will Mark You") — revisiting the brief already given in The Globe before lunch, this time with a chance for questions. Present Define instructions and model the 3 Whys on the board (AI Level 1 — no AI). Circulate as teams choose and lock in their problem.

Students: Get the full brief and judging criteria from your facilitator. Individually find THREE real-world problems (Journal p.3) and dig into one using the 3 Whys (Journal p.4). As a team, compare and lock in ONE shared problem (Workbook p.3–4). Begin individual organism research (Journal p.5).

Checkpoint 1 · By 3:20

What problem and possibilities are we exploring?

Confirm every team has locked in their problem, with reasons why it matters, before the day ends.

Students: Team problem locked in and signed off by every team member.

8:20–8:30 Tutor Rooms

Registration

Normal registration.

Morning Base Room

Learn & Explore

Present Learn & Explore instructions and model Parts • Purposes • Complexities (AURA Lab puffin-robot example). Remind teams: any AI-suggested fact about an organism MUST be checked against a real source (AI Level 3, Accuracy).

Students: Research organisms individually (Journal p.5–6). Request a STEAM token from your facilitator if specialist research support is needed.

10:00–10:20 · Break
Late morning Base Room

Research & Team Synthesis

Guide teams as they compare and pool their individual research.

Students: Compare organisms as a team (Workbook p.5); lock in the strongest biological inspiration (Workbook p.6); record key sources (Workbook p.7); complete the Nature → Function → Robot → Impact chain (Workbook p.8).

12:20–1:20 · Lunch
Checkpoint 2 · 1:20

What have we learned from nature?

Confirm every team member can explain the link between problem, organism and robot idea before moving on.

Students: Biological inspiration locked in; every team member can explain it.

Afternoon Base Room

Design & Choose

Present the Design Sprint instructions and the Official Design Checklist (AI Level 1 for the idea itself). Circulate during the Pugh Chart to make sure every idea is genuinely scored.

Students: Individually sketch and pitch your own robot idea (Journal p.8–9, no AI help). As a team, record every pitch (Workbook p.9), score all ideas in the Pugh Chart (Workbook p.10), lock in the final idea (Workbook p.11) and sketch the final design with three views (Workbook p.12).

Checkpoint 3 · 3:20

What are we going to build?

Confirm the final idea meets all 4 brief criteria (living organism / mimics anatomy / moves / specific function) and is locked in.

Students: Final design locked in and sketched, ready for tomorrow.

8:20–8:30 Tutor Rooms

Registration

Normal registration.

8:40–10:00 The Globe

Guest Speaker: Ms Tamika Daley-Baccus + Week Reminders

Bring your team to The Globe for the guest keynote, followed by an assembly-style talk on how to approach the rest of the week: using STEAM tokens, using the specialists and specialist zones, behaviour and participation expectations, and practising for the Expo stall. Scheduled to run until 10:00, but may finish early — be ready to bring your team back to their Base Room to begin Planning + Ready to Create if time remains before the break.

Students: Sit with your team in The Globe. Listen to the guest keynote, then to the reminders for the rest of the week. Head back to your Base Room with your facilitator when directed.

Before break, if time remains Base Room

Planning + Ready to Create

Work through the Ready to Create Approval Checklist with each team — materials, specialist needs, safety, feasibility. Sign off Workbook p.13 only once satisfied. If the Guest Speaker session runs the full 90 minutes, start this straight after the break instead.

Students: Finalise your materials and build plan; present it to your facilitator for approval before collecting any materials or accessing specialist zones.

10:00–10:20 · Break
Late morning Base Room / Specialist Zones

Create

Support building in the base room; issue STEAM tokens using the 5-step process for any team needing specialist equipment or space.

Students: Begin building your prototype; log progress in the Build Log (Workbook p.14–15, Journal p.10). Use a STEAM token if you need a specialist zone.

12:20–1:20 · Lunch
Afternoon Base Room / Specialist Zones

Create (continued)

Circulate and ask "what problem have you run into?" rather than "is it working?" — hitting problems is expected, not a failure.

Students: Continue building; solve emerging problems; keep the Build Log up to date. Videographer begins capturing footage for the process video (max 2 minutes).

Checkpoint 4 · 3:20

What have we created so far?

Confirm a prototype is genuinely in progress and the Build Log shows real problems and decisions, not just successes.

Students: Prototype in progress; Build Log up to date with at least one real problem and how it was handled.

8:20–8:30 Tutor Rooms

Registration

Normal registration.

Morning Base Room / Specialist Zones

Observe & Improve

Model the Ladder of Feedback (Clarify → Value → Concerns → Suggest) using the worked example.

Students: Test your prototype and collect real evidence (Journal p.11, Workbook p.16). Gather feedback from another team, a teacher or a visitor using the Ladder of Feedback (Workbook p.17).

10:00–10:20 · Break
Late morning Base Room / Specialist Zones

Improve & Re-test

Remind teams they don't have to act on every suggestion — deciding NOT to change something, for a good reason, is a valid decision too.

Students: Act on the feedback you choose to; modify your solution and re-test. Record a genuine Before → After comparison (Workbook p.18).

Checkpoint 5 · 12:20

What did we test, learn and improve?

Confirm teams collected real evidence (not just "it worked") and have a genuine Before/After.

Students: Testing evidence and Before/After comparison complete.

12:20–1:20 · Lunch
Afternoon Base Room

Reflect + Share

Present the Reflect instructions — protect this time, ONE reflection routine only. AI Level 1, no exceptions: reflection must be authentically the student's own.

Students: Individually complete What? So What? Now What? (Journal p.13) plus evidence of contribution and the 6Cs (Journal p.14). As a team, complete the Final Solution and Official Challenge Test (Workbook p.19) and What/So What/Now What (Workbook p.20); begin planning your 5-part Expo story (Workbook p.21–22).

Checkpoint 6 · 3:20

Are we ready to tell our story?

Confirm every student has completed their individual reflection and each team knows their 5-part story (Discover, Design, Create, Fail•Learn•Change, Final Solution).

Students: Individual reflections complete; team story planned and ready for tomorrow.

8:20–8:30 Tutor Rooms

Registration

Normal registration.

8:30–10:00 Sports Hall + Base Rooms

Finalise Projects & Expo Setup

Support teams finishing their displays; check the Poster, Workbook and video are ready to go on show.

Students: Finish your prototype, Poster, Workbook and video; set up your Expo stall (Workbook p.23). Run the Empty Stall Test on your own stall before it opens.

10:00–10:20 · Break
10:20–11:40 Sports Hall

MIT Week Expo

Supervise and enjoy the Expo. Judges use the marking rubric ("How Judges Will Mark You").

Students: Present your stall to visitors. Run the Official Challenge Test with a visitor — "can they tell what organism inspired you?" Year 6 visitors attend.

11:40–12:20 Sports Hall + Base Rooms + Specialist Zones

Pack Down & Tidy

Verify every space is 100% tidy per the Important Notice before releasing any team to The Globe.

Students: Tidy your stall, base room and any specialist areas completely; return all borrowed resources and STEAM tokens. Do not head to The Globe until your facilitator has checked your space.

12:20–1:20 · Lunch
1:20–2:40 The Globe

Final Celebration

Attend with your team.

Students: Celebrate the week's achievements together. House MIT Trophy and winners announced.

2:40–3:20 Base Room

Return to Team Bases

Lead wrap-up and get students to fill in reflections, or use this time to watch the uploaded videos from the other teams (bit.ly/mit26videoshare).

Students: Final reflection on the week; close of MIT Week 2026.

Key Reminders

The quick-reference version

Don't forget

  • STEAM Tokens — 2 per team from Monday. They justify the need to you; no token, no resource.
  • Award MIT Points via ePraise — it's your job to give them out, not just to watch for them. Award visibly, say why. See the Giving MIT Points guide above, or ask Sam if you need a refresher on logging it.
  • This is still teaching — bring your usual toolkit: Challenge, Explanation, Modelling, Practice & Feedback, Oracy.
  • Pace is flexible, readiness isn't — the only fixed point is every team ready for Friday's Expo, 10:20–11:40, Sports Hall.
  • Tidy space = MIT Points — a messy stall or base room can cost eligibility.
