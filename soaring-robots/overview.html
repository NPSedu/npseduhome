Overview

The challenge, at a glance

Part of Nord Anglia Education's MIT Week, this challenge brings a real MIT research area — biomimetic soaring flight — into the KS3 classroom.

Focus
Aerospace robotics

Soaring robots — gliders that stay aloft by riding rising air rather than burning fuel — are inspired by birds and used by researchers to study efficient, long-duration flight. In this challenge, students take on the role of aerospace engineers: they define a design problem, research how real soaring robots achieve lift and control, then design, build, and test their own glider.

The challenge is structured around a seven-stage engineering design cycle, run as a loop rather than a straight line — students are expected to revisit and improve their build as they learn what works.

Design cycle

Define → Learn & Explore → Design & Choose → Create → Observe & Improve → Reflect → Share

Every team moves through all seven stages — looping back into Create as many times as needed after Observe & Improve.

1 · Define

Pin down the problem: what must a soaring robot do, and what constraints — size, weight, materials — apply?

2 · Learn & Explore

Research how real gliders and MIT's soaring robots generate lift and stay stable in the air.

3 · Design & Choose

Sketch several concepts, weigh up the trade-offs, and choose the design most likely to fly.

4 · Create

Build a working prototype of the chosen design from the available materials.

5 · Observe & Improve

Test flight the prototype, record what happens, and identify what to change.

6 · Reflect

Evaluate what worked and why, connecting results back to engineering and aerodynamics principles.

7 · Share

Present the finished soaring robot and the design journey behind it to the class and judges.
